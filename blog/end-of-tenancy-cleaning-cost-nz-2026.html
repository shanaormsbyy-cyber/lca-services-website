How Much Does End of Tenancy Cleaning Cost in NZ? (2026 Price Guide)

Moving out of a rental and wondering what a professional bond clean will set you back? This guide covers current NZ cleaning prices by property size, what factors affect the cost, and whether DIY or professional cleaning makes more financial sense.

End of tenancy cleaning — also called a bond clean or exit clean — is one of the most common reasons tenants lose part or all of their bond. Getting the cleaning right is worth the investment. But what does a professional bond clean actually cost in 2026?

This guide is based on real pricing from LCA Services' end of tenancy cleaning and the NZ market as of early 2026. All prices exclude GST unless stated.

What's Included in a Standard Bond Clean?

A thorough end of tenancy clean should cover every surface and room that a property manager will check during their exit inspection. Here's what a complete bond clean includes:

  • Kitchen: Oven, rangehood, extractor fan, all benchtops, cupboards inside and out, splashback, sink, and dishwasher
  • Bathrooms & toilets: Full scrub including tiles, grout, toilet, vanity, mirror, shower screen, and extractor fan. Mould and calcium removal.
  • Bedrooms & living areas: Vacuuming, dusting, skirting boards, light fittings, window sills, curtain rails, and wardrobe interiors
  • Laundry: Washing machine wipe-down, dryer lint filter, and tub/sink clean
  • Windows: Interior window clean (exterior is usually an add-on)
  • Floors: Vacuum and mop all hard floors; vacuum carpets
  • General: All light switches, door handles, skirting boards, and cobweb removal throughout

What's NOT Included (and Costs Extra)

Most standard bond clean prices do not include the following — always confirm with your cleaner:

  • Exterior window cleaning — typically $80–$200 depending on property size and access
  • Carpet steam cleaning — $80–$300+ depending on area. Often required separately by NZ property managers.
  • Garage cleaning — $50–$150 depending on condition
  • Outdoor deck/patio cleaning — $80–$200
  • Flea treatment — required if pets were present; $200–$400 via a pest control company
  • Garden or lawn maintenance — not a cleaning service; hire a separate gardener

Key Factors That Affect Bond Clean Pricing

1. Property Size

This is the biggest driver. A studio takes 2–3 hours while a 4-bedroom house may take 8–10 hours. More rooms, more bathrooms, and more floor area directly increases the cost.

2. Property Condition

A clean, well-maintained property is much faster to prepare than one with heavy grease in the oven, calcium build-up on shower glass, or mould throughout the bathroom. Professional cleaners typically inspect and quote based on condition — expect to pay 20–40% more for a property in poor condition.

3. Location

Auckland prices tend to run slightly higher than Hamilton and Tauranga due to higher operating costs and demand. The difference is usually $50–$150 per job.

4. Add-Ons

Carpet steam cleaning, exterior windows, and garage cleaning are the most common add-ons and can add $150–$400 to a standard bond clean price.

5. How Long Since the Last Professional Clean

If a property has never been professionally cleaned during the tenancy, a more thorough initial deep clean is needed. This can increase pricing by 30–50% over a maintained property.

6. Hard Water / Glass Cancer

Properties in areas with hard water (common in the Bay of Plenty and parts of Auckland) can develop calcium or mineral staining on shower glass, often called "glass cancer." Removing this requires specialist treatment and adds $50–$150 to the bathroom portion of the clean.

Professional Bond Clean vs DIY — Is It Worth It?

Many tenants attempt to do their own bond clean to save money. Here's an honest comparison:

✅ Professional Bond Clean

  • Trained to PM inspection standards
  • Commercial-grade equipment (steam cleaners, industrial degreasers)
  • Typically takes 3–6 hours vs 1–2 days DIY
  • Re-clean guarantee if inspection fails
  • Photo documentation provided
  • Lower risk of bond deduction

⚠️ DIY Bond Clean

  • Saves money upfront — but risk of re-do costs
  • Consumer products less effective on heavy build-up
  • Time-consuming, especially oven and bathrooms
  • Easy to miss what property managers check
  • If inspection fails, you may need to hire a cleaner anyway
  • No documentation if landlord disputes arise

In NZ, the average bond is 4 weeks' rent. On a $600/week Auckland rental, that's $2,400 at stake. A professional bond clean at $600–$800 is a small fraction of your bond — and is tax-deductible if the property is an investment for your landlord (not relevant to you as a tenant, but it explains why landlords and PMs prefer professional cleans).

Bottom line: If you're moving out of a rental where you want your full bond back, the ROI on a professional bond clean is almost always positive. The risk of DIY isn't the upfront saving — it's the cost of re-cleaning, bond disputes, and time.

Do I Legally Have to Professionally Clean When Moving Out in NZ?

Under the Residential Tenancies Act 1986 (NZ), tenants are required to leave the property in a reasonably clean condition — but the law does not require a professional clean. However, many tenancy agreements include a clause requiring professional cleaning, particularly carpet steam cleaning.

Check your tenancy agreement carefully. If it states "professionally cleaned" or "steam cleaned," you are legally obligated to do so or the landlord can deduct it from your bond.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a bond clean cost for a 3-bedroom house in Auckland?

A 3-bedroom bond clean in Auckland typically costs $750–$950 depending on condition. LCA Services prices a 3-bedroom at $800 (excluding GST).

How long does a bond clean take?

A 1-bedroom property typically takes 3–4 hours. A 3-bedroom house takes 6–8 hours. A team of 2 cleaners can often halve the time.

Can I get a bond back without a professional clean?

Yes — if you clean thoroughly yourself and the property passes inspection, you can get your full bond back without a professional clean. However, many tenants underestimate what a property manager checks. High-risk areas include: oven interior, rangehood, shower glass (calcium), extractor fans, and skirting boards.

What is the difference between an end of tenancy clean and a regular clean?

An end of tenancy clean is a deep clean to inspection standard — it covers areas that regular cleaning skips, such as inside ovens, tops of doors, skirting boards, window tracks, light fittings, and extractor fans. It typically takes 2–3x longer than a regular maintenance clean.
